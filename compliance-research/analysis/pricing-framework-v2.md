The first pricing analysis was built on data from the wrong companies. Of 35 companies scanned:

- The "enterprise" and "large" tiers ($8K+ audits, $10K+ floors) are irrelevant

The target market is:
- **Healthcare practices**: 3–50 employees, $400K–$5M revenue
- **Boutique hotels**: 10–80 employees, $2M–$15M revenue
- **Single-location auto dealers**: 30–80 employees, $20M–$80M gross (but 2–3% net margins = $400K–$2.4M actual disposable income)
- **Single senior care facilities**: 20–60 employees, $2M–$8M revenue
- **Small/mid DTC e-commerce**: 10–60 employees, $2M–$30M revenue

These are businesses where the **owner or office manager** makes the buying decision — not a VP of Digital or a Legal department. They think in monthly expenses, not annual budgets.

## The Business Owner's Mental Math

A small business owner doesn't compare your price to their annual revenue.
They compare it to **what they already spend per month** on things they consider optional.

| Expense | Typical Monthly Cost | Decision Speed |
|---|---|---|
| Rent / lease | $5,000–$15,000 | Committed |
| Digital marketing (Google Ads, SEO) | $1,000–$5,000 | Medium — they track ROI |
| IT / website hosting | $50–$500 | Fast — it's small |
| Accounting / bookkeeping | $500–$2,000 | Fast — they need it |
| Legal retainer | $500–$3,000 | Medium |
| Insurance | $500–$2,000 | Committed |

**Key insight**: An audit under $1,500 sits in the same mental bucket as "one month of accounting."
That's a one-conversation close. An audit at $3,000+ enters "let me think about it" territory.

For the **fix**, the comparison is different. The owner thinks:
"Is this cheaper than what I'd pay to redo my website entirely?"
If the fix costs more than ~60% of a new website, the rational answer is "just build me a new one."

## Does the $10,000 Auto Dealer Audit Make Sense?

**No.** Here's why:

The $10,000 number was calculated for Ken Garff Automotive — a $3B–$5B company with 4,000–6,000 employees. That's not your market.

A single-location car dealership:
- Revenue: $20M–$60M gross, but **net margins are 2–3%** → $400K–$1.8M actual profit
- They spend $3K–$10K/month on digital advertising
- Their website is often a managed platform (Dealer.com, DealerInspire, DDC)
- The owner/GM makes website decisions
- A $10,000 audit is **6–12 months of their current website costs**

At $1,000–$1,500, the audit is "try it and see." At $10,000, the answer is "no."

**The same logic applies to every industry when you limit to <100 employees.** These businesses don't have a "compliance budget." They have an owner who either says "yeah, let's do it" or "not right now." Price for the first response.
