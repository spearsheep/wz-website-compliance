# B2B & Professional Services — ADA Web Accessibility Lawsuits
## Research Summary — Run 2 (May 21, 2026)

## Overview

This run focused on ADA web accessibility enforcement actions against B2B and professional services organizations. Unlike the B2C-heavy public lawsuit databases, B2B enforcement is characterized by (1) DOJ-driven consent decrees and settlements rather than private class actions, (2) structured negotiations that never reach a court docket, and (3) NFB-led enforcement campaigns targeting healthcare and professional licensing infrastructure.

**Total B2B cases documented:** 11 case files (10 unique defendants/events)  
**Cases with Tier 1 primary sources and confirmed dockets:** 7  
**Cases recommended for use in report:** 8

## Strongest 3–5 Hero Anchor Cases

### 1. United States v. MedStar Health, Inc. — $440,000 Consent Decree (2024)
**File:** `b2b-lawsuits/doj-v-medstar-health.json`  
**Court:** D. Maryland, 1:24-cv-00302 | **Resolved:** February 5, 2024  
**Sources (Tier 1):** DOJ press release, CRT case page, signed complaint PDF, consent decree PDF, USAO-MD press release, Civil Rights Litigation Clearinghouse  

The DOJ filed a complaint and simultaneous consent decree against MedStar Health — Maryland and DC's largest integrated hospital system — requiring $440,000 in compensation to affected patients. The decree explicitly mandated that MedStar publish all compliance information on a WCAG 2.1 AA-compliant website. This is the most recent and largest named DOJ healthcare enforcement action.

**Why it's a hero anchor:** Named defendant, confirmed docket, DOJ Tier 1 sources, $440K disclosed settlement, WCAG 2.1 AA web compliance required.

### 2. Bone et al. v. UNC Health Care System — $125,000 + Federal Injunction (2018–2023)
**File:** `b2b-lawsuits/bone-v-unc-health-care.json`  
**Court:** M.D.N.C., 1:18-cv-00994 | **Resolved:** 2023 (permanent injunction)  
**Sources (Tier 1):** Disability Rights NC press release, Civil Rights Litigation Clearinghouse, CourtListener docket, FindLaw case text  

A 5-year litigation campaign by the NFB and Disability Rights NC against UNC Health Care System — one of North Carolina's largest health systems — for systematically denying blind patients accessible after-visit summaries, billing statements, medication instructions, and patient portal access. $125,000 damages settled July 2022. A federal judge then issued a 109-page permanent injunction in June 2023 requiring system-wide overhaul of all patient communications.

**Why it's a hero anchor:** Named defendant, confirmed docket, $125K disclosed, court-ordered injunction, 5-year litigation showing escalation risk.

---

### 3. DOJ v. Springfield Clinic — Patient Portal Overhaul Forced (2024)
**File:** `b2b-lawsuits/doj-v-springfield-clinic-b2b.json`  
**DOJ Reference:** DJ 202-24-114 | **Announced:** May 8, 2024  
**Sources (Tier 1):** DOJ USAO Central District of Illinois press release, signed settlement agreement PDF  

DOJ forced Springfield Clinic (one of Illinois' largest independent physician groups) to make its patient portal (FollowMyHealth), medical records portal (CIOX), website, and mobile apps all WCAG 2.1 AA compliant within 9 months. The complaint arose from a single patient who had to call the clinic and have personal medical information read aloud over the phone because the online portal was inaccessible. Annual DOJ reporting required.

**Why it's a hero anchor:** DOJ enforcement, specific WCAG 2.1 AA mandate on patient portals and third-party vendors (FollowMyHealth, CIOX), small-to-mid healthcare defendant, direct web accessibility case.

### 4. AICPA & NASBA — CPA Exam Accessibility (DOJ, 2019)
**File:** `b2b-lawsuits/aicpa-nasba-cpa-exam-doj.json`  
**Court:** U.S. Attorney's Office, D. Massachusetts (administrative) | **Resolved:** April 1, 2019  
**Sources (Tier 1):** DOJ archive.ada.gov settlement text, USAO Massachusetts press release  

The DOJ forced the AICPA (the professional standards body for the entire US accounting profession) and NASBA (the licensing body) to make the Uniform CPA Exam compatible with JAWS screen readers and ZoomText Magnifier after a legally blind candidate requested accessible testing accommodations and was offered only a human reader. $15,000 paid to the complainant plus payments to other aggrieved persons.

**Why it's a hero anchor:** The "irony angle" — the accounting profession's own gatekeepers had inaccessible digital infrastructure. DOJ Tier 1 sources. Named organizations. Confirmed $15,000+ settlement. Biannual compliance reporting.

### 5. Schwab — WCAG 2.2 AA for Full Wealth Management Platform (2024)
**File:** `b2b-lawsuits/schwab-blind-clients-structured-negotiation.json`  
**Effective Date:** August 26, 2024  
**Sources (Tier 1):** Law Office of Lainey Feingold (primary negotiating counsel, published full agreement summary)  

Four blind Charles Schwab retail clients (represented by two leading accessibility attorneys) secured a structured negotiation agreement requiring Schwab to achieve WCAG 2.2 AA compliance across schwab.com, Schwab Mobile, and Thinkorswim — the highest WCAG standard currently available. Quarterly usability testing by persons with disabilities required. Vendors must achieve compliance within 30 days of request. Most significant recent financial services resolution.

**Why it's a hero anchor:** Enterprise financial services defendant, WCAG 2.2 AA (highest standard), vendor compliance obligations, quarterly disability testing requirement — signals where the industry is heading.

## Healthcare-Specific HHS Post-2024 Ruling Context

The HHS Section 504 final rule was published May 9, 2024 and took effect July 8, 2024. Key facts for the report:

- **All organizations receiving HHS funding** (virtually every US hospital, clinic, pharmacy, medical group) must make websites, web content, and mobile apps WCAG 2.1 AA compliant.
- **Compliance deadlines extended** (May 2026 Interim Final Rule): Large organizations (15+ employees) → May 11, 2027. Small organizations (<15 employees) → May 10, 2028.
- **CRITICAL:** The extension covers only the technical WCAG compliance deadline. The underlying Section 504 obligation to provide accessible programs and services has been enforceable since July 8, 2024. A patient who cannot access a patient portal or scheduling tool today has a Section 504 claim today.
- **OCR can investigate proactively** without a complaint. For Medicare-participating hospitals, OCR can suspend or terminate federal financial assistance — an existential threat far exceeding typical ADA lawsuit settlements.
- **Private right of action** under Section 504 remains available throughout the extension period.

Sources: HHS press release (src-040), Duane Morris analysis, adaquickscan.com.

## Law Firm Cases — The Irony Angle

Research found **no confirmed cases of law firms being sued as defendants for their own inaccessible websites** with Tier 1 sources (named defendant, confirmed docket, published outcome). However:

- ADA Title III explicitly lists "offices of lawyers" as a covered place of public accommodation (42 U.S.C. §12181(7)(F))
- An accessibility study found that 5 of the top 10 plaintiff law firms (those that sue others for accessibility violations) use accessibility overlay widgets on their own sites — the same widgets they argue provide no legal protection
- No published federal case against a named law firm as defendant for website inaccessibility was found in open sources
- Law firms are likely settling via demand letter before court filing; most demand letters never produce a public record

**Gap note:** PACER search would likely surface cases — this is a research gap that requires paid legal database access.

## Identified Gaps and Notes for Further Research

1. **PACER access needed:** Financial services RIA cases, credit union cases, and law firm defendant cases are almost certainly filed but not appearing in open-source searches. A PACER search for ADA + website + "financial advisor" or "law firm" in SDNY/NDCA/SDFL would yield specific named defendants.

2. **Law firm defendant angle is unconfirmed:** The "lawyers being sued" narrative is highly compelling but currently unsupported by Tier 1 sources. This is the single highest-priority gap.

3. **Accounting firm defendants:** No specific accounting firm (Big 4 or mid-tier) was found as a named website accessibility defendant in public sources. The AICPA case covers the certification body, not individual accounting firms. Gap remains.

4. **Commercial real estate, consulting, staffing:** No specific named defendants found in public sources. These sectors appear to resolve entirely via demand letter.

5. **HHS OCR enforcement actions:** As of May 2026, HHS OCR has not published specific Section 504 digital accessibility enforcement actions against named healthcare providers since the July 2024 effective date. The May 2026 extension suggests OCR is not yet in active enforcement mode — but the private right of action is live now.

6. **Epic Systems dismissal significance:** The judge's ruling that a B2B software vendor cannot be held liable for selling inaccessible software to employers creates a potential shield for B2B technology vendors — but does not protect the healthcare employer/customer who buys and deploys the inaccessible software.
